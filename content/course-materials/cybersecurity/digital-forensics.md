# Digital Forensics

_Cybersecurity · Faculty of Technology · Level 100L_

## Overview

Digital Forensics teaches students how digital evidence is identified, preserved, examined, and documented during investigations. Topics include evidence integrity, forensic imaging, file-system analysis, metadata, event logs, and investigation procedures. Students learn why accurate documentation and chain of custody matter, while considering privacy, legal requirements, and the limitations of forensic conclusions.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:

- Explain the main ideas that make up digital forensics.
- Describe evidence integrity and how it is used.
- Describe forensic imaging and how it is used.
- Describe file-system analysis and how it is used.
- Describe metadata and how it is used.
- Describe event logs and how it is used.
- Apply the concepts to a small, realistic task.
- Recognise common mistakes and know how to avoid them.

## Introduction

Cybersecurity focuses on protecting computers, networks, applications, and information from unauthorized access, misuse, and digital threats. Students learn security principles, risk assessment, defensive technologies, incident response, and ethical practices for maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and availability of digital systems.

In this subject, the goal is not just to memorise facts but to build a reliable way of thinking about digital forensics. Work through each lesson in order, try the examples, and answer the review questions before moving on. If a term is new, write a one-line definition in your own words — that habit alone will make the material stick.
