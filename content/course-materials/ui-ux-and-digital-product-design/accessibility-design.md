# Accessibility Design

_UI/UX and Digital Product Design · Faculty of Technology · Level 100L_

## Overview

Accessibility Design teaches students how to make digital products usable by people with diverse abilities and needs. Topics include keyboard navigation, colour contrast, readable content, semantic structure, captions, and assistive technologies. Practical evaluations identify barriers and recommend improvements, helping learners create more inclusive experiences and understand accessibility standards and user diversity.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:

- Explain the main ideas that make up accessibility design.
- Describe keyboard navigation and how it is used.
- Describe colour contrast and how it is used.
- Describe readable content and how it is used.
- Describe semantic structure and how it is used.
- Describe captions and how it is used.
- Apply the concepts to a small, realistic task.
- Recognise common mistakes and know how to avoid them.

## Introduction

UI/UX and Digital Product Design focuses on creating digital products that are visually clear, accessible, and easy to use. Students learn user research, interface design, prototyping, usability evaluation, interaction principles, and design systems to improve how people interact with software.

In this subject, the goal is not just to memorise facts but to build a reliable way of thinking about accessibility design. Work through each lesson in order, try the examples, and answer the review questions before moving on. If a term is new, write a one-line definition in your own words — that habit alone will make the material stick.
